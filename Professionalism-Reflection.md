# Professionalism Reflection

## Research and Learn

### Professional vs unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment

| Area | Professional | Unprofessional |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Clear and polite messages, replying within a reasonable time | Ignoring messages, rude or sarcastic replies |
| Meetings | Joining on time, being prepared, listening, taking notes | Joining late, multitasking, interrupting others |
| Teamwork | Sharing updates, helping others, owning my mistakes | Blaming others, hiding problems, going silent on a task |
| Humour | Friendly jokes that include everyone | Jokes about someone's gender, race, religion, disability or background |
| Work | Finishing what I said I would do, or saying early if I cannot | Missing deadlines without telling anyone |

A casual culture means I can be relaxed and be myself. It does not mean I can be careless or disrespectful.

### Respectful communication in remote teams

- Text has no tone of voice, so I should write clearly and politely, and read my message once before sending.
- Assume good intent when a message sounds short or blunt.
- Disagree with the idea, not the person. Explain my reason and listen to theirs.
- If a discussion is getting heated in text, move it to a short call.
- Respect time zones and working hours, and do not expect instant replies.
- Thank people and give credit for their work.
- When I get feedback, say thanks and act on it instead of arguing.

### Giving and receiving feedback professionally

Giving feedback:

- Talk about the work or the action, not the person
- Be specific and give an example
- Suggest how it could be improved
- Give critical feedback in private, and praise in public

Receiving feedback:

- Listen fully before replying
- Assume the person wants to help me
- Ask questions if something is not clear, instead of getting defensive
- Say thank you, make the change, and let them know when it is done

### Key points from the guide on working with supervisors

- I am expected to become more self-led over time. I should not wait to be told what to do, but I also should not go off on my own. If I am unsure, I can say what I think the next step is and ask if that is right.
- Come to meetings prepared, with an agenda and questions. Take notes and follow up on my action items.
- Do not multitask in meetings.
- Do a quick search before asking basic questions.
- Give an update at least once at the end of each working day.
- If I am stuck, search online first, then ask a teammate, and if I am still blocked after 30 minutes, reach out to my supervisor.
- Use the right channel based on urgency: email for general things, Discord for medium priority, SMS for urgent things, and a call only for a real emergency.
- Calendar invites should have the project name and a short agenda.

## Reflection

### Great teamwork and professionalism I have seen

In my uni group projects, the ones that went well were the ones where we split the work clearly at the start, so everyone knew what they owned. People gave short updates in the group chat, and if someone was stuck, they said it early instead of hiding it until the deadline. Nobody blamed anyone when something went wrong, we just fixed it together. What made it effective was clear ownership, regular updates and honesty.

### Making my communication clear, respectful and inclusive

- Keep messages short, with one main point
- Say exactly what I need and by when
- Use simple words, and avoid slang, idioms and sarcasm
- Read my message again before sending it
- Use bullet points or steps for longer messages
- Listen without interrupting, and make sure everyone gets a chance to speak
- Ask people how they prefer to communicate, and respect it

### Helping create a positive, fun and professional work environment

- Be friendly and greet people
- Appreciate others' work and celebrate small wins
- Join in on the fun, but keep jokes kind and inclusive
- Help teammates when they are stuck
- Be reliable and do what I said I would do
- Speak up politely if I see something that is not okay

### Preparing for meetings with supervisors and colleagues

- Read the brief or the related issue before the meeting
- Write down my progress, my blockers and my questions
- Send or bring a short agenda
- Look up basic things myself first, so I can ask better questions
- Join on time, and do not multitask
- Take notes, and follow up on my action items after the meeting

### Being proactive during my internship

- Not wait for instructions. When I finish a task, I will pick the next one or suggest what I think I should do next.
- Give an update at the end of each working day, even if it is just "still working on it"
- Flag blockers early
- Try to solve a problem myself first, then ask a teammate, then my supervisor
- Suggest improvements when I notice something, like the `.env` format mistake I found in the setup README
- Ask for feedback instead of waiting for it

### Following up and escalating

If I need information or an action from someone:

1. Send a clear message on the right channel, saying what I need and by when.
2. If there is no reply within the expected time, send a polite reminder on the same channel.
3. If there is still no reply, move to a more urgent channel.

The channels and expected reply times are:

- Email: general things, reply within 48 hours
- Discord: medium priority, reply within 24 hours
- SMS: urgent, reply within 2 hours
- Call: real emergency only

I would escalate when the reply time has passed and the work is blocked or a deadline is close. I would start with the lightest option first, because most things are not urgent. If my supervisor is not reachable and it is really urgent, I would ask another team lead.

### Things I will not do

- Wait around for instructions
- Come to meetings unprepared
- Skip taking notes and then ask the same question again
- Ask questions that I could find the answer to in 10 seconds
- Go silent on my project without any updates
- Ignore feedback or action items
- Rely on only one channel when something is urgent
- Send calendar invites with unclear subjects
- Bully, discriminate, or make jokes that put someone down
- Share confidential information outside the team
