# Competitive Landscape

## Apps I looked at

| App | What it mainly does | Platforms | Payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focus Bear | Blocking + morning/evening routines + breaks, built for ADHD and autism | Mac, Windows, iOS, Android | 7 day free trial, then subscription |
| Freedom | Blocks websites, apps or the whole internet, synced across devices | Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, Chromebook | Free tier, then subscription |
| Cold Turkey | Very strict blocker for websites, apps or the whole computer | Windows, Mac | One-time payment |
| Tiimo | Visual daily planner with a colour-coded timeline, made for ADHD and autism | iOS, Android, web, Apple Watch | Free tier, then subscription |
| Routinery | Step-by-step routine timer and habit tracker | iOS, Android, Apple Watch | Free with paid premium |

Quick summary of each:

- **Freedom** is good at blocking across all devices at once and has a locked mode so you can't stop a session early. It does not help with routines or habits, and some reviews say it is easy to turn off.
- **Cold Turkey** is the strictest blocker. Once a block is locked, you can't change it or uninstall the app. It is a one-time purchase, which is nice, but it only works on computers and does not do routines or breaks.
- **Tiimo** is a visual planner with a timeline of the day, timers for each task, and AI that breaks tasks into smaller steps. It is also made for ADHD and autism, but it plans your day rather than blocking distractions.
- **Routinery** guides you through routines step by step with a timer for each step and voice alerts. It is good for building habits, but it does not block anything.

## What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?

Focus Bear is the only one that puts blocking and routines together in one app. With the others, I would need Freedom or Cold Turkey for blocking, and Tiimo or Routinery for routines.

Some other things that are different:

- It is built by a team that is neurodivergent themselves, specifically for ADHD and autism.
- It takes over the computer for the morning and evening routine, so it actually pushes you to do your habits, not just remind you.
- It has micro breaks with stretching, and it can tell when you are in a meeting and stay quiet.
- It uses AI to decide if a website is related to your task, instead of only using a fixed block list.
- The tone is gentle and not shaming, for example Cuddly Bear mode asks before blocking.

## If I were a user, why would I choose Focus Bear over competitors?

If I had ADHD and struggled with both distractions and routines, I would choose Focus Bear because it handles both in one place, so I would not need to pay for and set up two apps. The breaks and wind-down reminders also help with burnout and hyperfocus, which the other apps don't really cover.

To be honest, if I only wanted a strict blocker for my laptop, Cold Turkey might be a better choice since it is stricter and is a one-time payment. If I only wanted to plan my day visually, Tiimo looks easier to use.

## What is one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?

Tiimo has a visual timeline that shows the whole day in colour-coded blocks, with AI that breaks big tasks into smaller steps. Focus Bear shows one habit at a time and has a to-do list, but it does not have a visual view of the whole day. For people with ADHD who have time blindness, seeing the day laid out can really help.

## What is one improvement Focus Bear could make?

Make the first-time setup simpler. Focus Bear has a lot of settings (block lists, strictness levels, bear modes, Pomodoro, routines, wind-down time), which can be overwhelming for a new user, especially someone with ADHD. The company also lists improving the onboarding experience as a key priority.

My idea is to offer a few ready-made starter setups, like "Student", "Work from home" or "Just block distractions", that set everything up in one click. The user could then change the details later once they are comfortable.
